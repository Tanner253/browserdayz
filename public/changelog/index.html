Ten new guns are in the Zone: two .50 rifles, a silenced 12.7 mm sniper rifle, the short AK, the MP7 and the Scorpion, a double-barrel shotgun, and three pairs held one in each hand. The M32 grenade launcher is for admins. Every gun aims through its own sights now, scopes zoom on the mouse wheel, and the village is tier 3 loot.

The Scorpion, the Dual SMGs and the Dual MAC-10s in Kamenka and the village (tier 3) and under the gas. The MP7 and the AKS-74U under the gas (tier 4). The VSSK, the .50 sniper rifle and the Barrett in Bunker 17 (tier 5). The fort's racks can hold the VSSK, the .50s, the AKS-74U and the MP7.
  • Their parts: the MP7's own sight and suppressor, the MAC-10s' suppressor, and the Elcan for the AR-15. The foregrip is now the M32's vertical grip, and it fits the AR-15, the MPA30 and the double-barrel.
  • Their rounds: .50 BMG and 12.7x55 lie in tens under the gas and in the bunker, and in cans of 25 in the bunker.
  • The Barrett's bipod: B lets it down or folds it away.
  • The village is tier 3 now, as Kamenka is: submachine guns, armoured vests, sights, and gas masks at its police station. Tier 2 is the checkpoints, the army camp, the depot and the market. There is more loot on the map than before.

The map, three times bigger

The Zone now runs 4.6 km west to east, three times the old 1.5 km square. Past the old valley lies a green west of rivers, a lake and a swamp, with five new towns. Beyond it a red canyon ridge closes the whole map, crossed by one pass. Past that is a desert with the ruins of Kalat, a beach and the sea. Everybody starts again at the map's edge with what they carried.

  • The green west: farmland, woods and a river valley between the old valley and the ridge. Ozerki's lake and the swamp are in it, and you can wade in the water.
  • Five new towns: Pokrov (22 buildings), Lipki (13), Ozerki (8), Mokroye (7), and Zavod, the brick works (3). Zastava, the post at the pass's east mouth, adds 5 more. Loot runs up to tier 3 in Pokrov, Zavod and Zastava, and tier 2 in Lipki, Ozerki and Mokroye.
  • The pass: the ridge cannot be crossed anywhere else. The pass is 517 m long, with shelves up both walls at about 8, 18 and 30 m. Six caves run through those walls, and the best of the pass is cached on its floor at the middle. The caves hold tier-4 loot, the cache tier 5.
  • The desert: dunes and buttes, the river running through it to the sea, and the ruins of Kalat: tier-3 loot in its streets and walls, tier 4 in its citadel.
  • Moved: Bunker 17 and its gas now sit on the river valley's south rim, well away from the fort. The trader has moved to the main road.
  • The map (M): the land is in colour now: green country, desert sand, red cliffs, snow, blue water. Every hundred-metre square has its name in its corner, A to AT across and 1 to 15 down, so you can say "I'm in C12". The grid lines are stronger.
  • Draw distance: a new setting from 300 to 1,500 m (800 by default) for how far buildings are drawn. Loot is drawn within 55 m whatever it is set to.
  • Smoother walking: pictures of things you walk past now go onto the graphics card a little each frame, not all at once in one frame.
  • Fixed: a player shot out of a helicopter no longer hangs in the sky. The body rides in its seat until the helicopter lands or crashes, then lies beside it to be searched. The airdrop's helicopter now flies in over the map's real edge.

The helicopter's searchlight reaches the ground

The helicopter's light was a car's headlight pointed straight ahead: from the air it lit nothing below. It is a police helicopter's searchlight now, a narrow beam from under the nose that throws a pool of light on the ground from 50 m up all the way to the 100 m ceiling, and the beam itself shows in the night air.

  • Like a police helicopter's: they carry searchlights such as the Nightsun, whose beam is focused between 4 and 20 degrees wide. Ours is 17 degrees: a pool about 13 m across from 50 m up and about 23 m from 90 m.
  • As bright high as low: the spot is as bright at the ceiling as at 50 m, only wider.
  • Steady: it points down and a little ahead of where the helicopter is going, and stays there when the nose dips to fly or it leans into a turn.
  • Seen from the ground: the lens is a bright star to whoever it is turned toward, and the beam is a pale shaft in the night air. A helicopter out looking for you is seen across the valley, as any lit lamp is.
  • When: after dark, while somebody is at the controls, as a car's headlights are.

Bot farms turned away at the door, and a dropped connection takes you straight back in

On 16 October a dozen made-up names (Merric635, Brambl116, Drayto518 and the rest) came through about fifteen rented addresses in many countries, every one of them the same Chrome on the same graphics card, passed the bot check, were sent back out of the world within seconds and came round again every half minute, with new names and keys each half hour. Your game now tells the server what it runs on, as one hash, and the server knows one machine under a dozen names for what it is. Nothing to do and nothing shown: it takes a few tens of milliseconds the first time you press Play. And a connection that drops takes you straight back to your body.

  • What your game tells the server: a single hash of how your graphics card draws a small test picture, how your browser draws a line of text, your screen, time zone, languages, cores, memory and system. Not your name, not where you are, and nothing that changes while you play (not the window's size, not the page's zoom). Two of the same machine set up the same way look the same, so no rule turns anybody away for the device alone.
  • Kept off by device: a device whose games are sent back to the bot check four times in half an hour is kept off for a day, whatever those games call themselves and wherever they come from. Before this it was the name and the address that were kept off, and the swarm changed both every half hour.
  • A farm: three games on one device from three different addresses inside an hour, and the device is kept off for a day and its games put out. One player whose phone changes its address as they play is one game, however many addresses; two friends on the same kind of laptop are two games. Neither is a farm.
  • Two games to a device at once. Two on one computer at home is fine. A third is told so, and is in when one of the two leaves.
  • Before the bot check: all of this is asked the moment the game says hello, so a farm spends nothing of the course, and no body of theirs ever stands in the world.
  • For admins: the device is on /players, /recent and the panel (dev: and eight characters); /same dev:xxxxxxxx lists everybody playing or lately gone on it; /ban dev:xxxxxxxx bans the computer, not the name, and it holds for every name on it. A wrong call is on /bans and /unban lifts it.
  • Sent back four times in half an hour, by name and by address, is kept off for a day as well: a swarm that passes the course and is thrown out of the world again and again no longer gets a fresh go each time.
  • A card named without its number fails the bot check: every real Chrome on Windows names its graphics card with its id, as in "RTX 3060 (0x00002504)"; over two and a half days of the server's log every player's did, and every one of the swarm's said "RTX 3060" with none. A typed string, not a card.
  • Too many shots: a go at the bot check ends at 90 shots (the people who passed on 16 October fired 9 to 45; the swarm's programs 63 to 160), and a dozen or more shots that land by the geometry but were not aimed by a hand fail the go.
  • Pardon: the admin panel's Bans page lists everybody turned away at the door lately (banned, kept off, a full device, the bot check not passed) with a Pardon button that lifts everything on that person at once, their game, address and device, so a wrong call is undone in one press. /turned and /pardon in the chat do the same.
  • Bans written reliably: a ban always held the moment it was given, but the site's copy of the list (which outlasts restarts) was refused for about a hundred seconds after any write, and the admin was told it would hold only until a restart. The server now hands the site the mark of what it last wrote, and keeps trying for ten minutes if the site cannot take it. Somebody banned also leaves the panel's "Left lately" list.
  • Dropped? You're back in: a connection that drops in the middle of play reconnects by itself, straight to your body where you left it, with everything you carried; no reload. How each connection ended is written down in the server's log, so a drop can be traced.
  • The infected leave a dropped player's body alone while its connection is gone.
  • Sound: a car's or a helicopter's own sound is heard from where your ears are, not swung round your head; the sound starts with any key you press, not only a click.
  • AR ammo: 5.56 rounds are found by rate wherever the police and the army were, not at a handful of set spots.
  • The chat frees the mouse while you type, to point at what was said and scroll back, and takes it again when the chat shuts.
  • Fixed: one scope or light fitted to many guns from one item; bodies left standing in the sky over a house; an admin's spare things dropped in the sky; a helicopter stood under a roof; an admin's /spawn lands at their own height, on a roof as on the ground.

Play with a controller

A PlayStation or Xbox controller, on a PC or an iPad. Switch it on in Settings, under Controls: its buttons are where Call of Duty has them.

  • The buttons: R2 fires, L2 aims. ✕ jumps; ○ crouches, held lies down. □ reloads, or takes, opens a door or searches when there is something in front of you. △ is the next weapon, held empties your hands. L3 sprints, and aiming holds your breath. R3 is the light; L1 and R1 lean. On the D-pad: up the map, down the call-out wheel (held, the right stick picks), left single shots or automatic (held, drops what you hold), right looks the gun over (held, talks). The touchpad, or View on Xbox, is the inventory; Options is the menu.
  • In a vehicle: the left stick drives, L3 sounds the horn and R3 the siren. In the helicopter ✕ climbs and ○ drops.
  • Settings: how fast the right stick looks round, and the look turned up and down round, under Controller.
  • On an iPad or a phone the touch buttons go away while the controller is used, and come back at a touch.
  • The emote wheel says Click to edit in its middle. Opening Emotes from it no longer brings up the menu as well.

The Arena opens: team matches at the fort

A second way to play. Team against team at Orlinoye, in rounds as in CS:GO: a round ends when one side has nobody left standing, and the first side to the rounds chosen wins the match. Open the Wagers tab on the menu to host a match, join one, watch one, or practise alone.

  • Host a match: 1v1, 2v2 or 3v3; first to 3, 5 or 10 rounds; and one loadout for everybody in it, every round: Assault, Heavy, Marksman or Breacher.
  • Guns only, and they never run dry. No magazines, rounds, armour or food to see to.
  • The map is the level ground the fort stands on, from the foot of the mountain to the edge of the cliff. One side starts before the gate, the other behind the back wall; the fort is between them, and every way through it.
  • The arena's own server decides every hit and every point of health. No game's word on either is taken.
  • A screen of its own: the score and who is left standing at the top, kills at the top right, health and the gun at the foot, and Tab for the scoreboard.
  • Watch any match being played from the Wagers tab, flying and unseen, with the score and the kills as they happen, and the result at the end.
