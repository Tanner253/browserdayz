  • Report a death: on the death screen press R or Report, pick a reason and say what happened; the admins get it with how you died.

  • Your party: its members on the maps and the compass, with their names over their heads; party chat (Tab in the chat box to Party); and a bed or sleeping bag given to a member (look at it and hold G; tap G to pick whom).
  • No hiding by tabbing out: a body on the server can always be killed, whatever its game is doing. Tabbed out a minute, your body stays where you stood for five minutes, as when a connection drops, and a game that stops taking its hits is killed by the server.
  • A dropped connection keeps your seat, in a car or a helicopter, and your game comes back into your body on its own without the captcha again: a pilot no longer comes back in the air and falls.
  • Hold the button to keep chopping and mining. Every knife skins an animal, and every sharp thing, a hatchet, a machete, a saw, cuts clothes into rags, a survivor's own shirts and jackets too. A forge's charcoal waits for room instead of being lost.

  • For the admins: a search of everybody who has played, online or not, with a ban that holds whether they are on or not; every report from the death screen with how that death went; what a banned cheat script ran, shown with its ban; and every chat, party and nearby included, can be read by the admins to deal with abuse.

Animals and hunting: red deer, wolves, bears and cows

Red deer, wolf packs, bears and farm cows now live in the wilds, away from towns and roads. Hunt them, gut them with a knife, and cook the meat: eaten raw it makes you ill as often as not.

  • Where they live: in the wilds, 400 m and more from towns, 250 m from camps, 150 m from lone buildings (hunting towers excepted) and 100 m from roads. Deer keep to forest and meadow, never the high snow; cows stay on their farms; the three bears on the map each live alone, far apart.
  • Red deer graze in the open at dawn and dusk and lie up in the trees by day. A shot or a running survivor sends the whole herd off together, barking. A stag roars at dusk and first light.
  • Wolves hunt as DayZ's do: packs of three to five. Once a pack has found you it walks in, circles, and sends one wolf in at a time, which stops and snarls before it lunges. You cannot outrun them. Kill three of it (two of a pack of three) and the rest run; shoot at it as it runs and it turns back.
  • Wolves also get hungry. A fed pack lets you pass unless you come within 9 m. A hungry one locks on with a howl, from 30 m by day and 70 m at night. It hunts deer too: it pulls one down, the whole pack eats it where it fell for three minutes, and it drives off anybody who comes near.
  • Bears: a bear warns you with a roar, then charges, swipes and bites. Run at once from more than a dozen metres off and it will not catch you; out of its sight it soon gives up, and under a roof you are safe.
  • Hunting: gut and skin a body with a knife in six seconds. A stag gives 7 venison steaks, 2 fat, a hide and 5 bones (a hind 5 steaks); a bear gives 10 steaks, 6 fat, a pelt and 8 bones. A machete or a hatchet gives less. A carcass the wolves have eaten gives a third of its meat, half its fat and no hide.
  • Cooking: a steak cooks in a minute at a lit fire or a stove, and burns 45 seconds after that. Firewood, from sheds, barns and hunters' cabins, builds a fire where you stand. Raw meat goes off in 40 minutes, cooked meat in three hours.
  • Food poisoning: you throw up every half minute or so, losing water and food, for five minutes. Charcoal tablets end it at once. Multivitamins, from medical places and homes, halve the chance of falling ill for twenty minutes.
